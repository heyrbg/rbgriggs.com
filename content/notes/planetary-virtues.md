Virtue ethics has an obvious technological problem, and it is a problem of scale.

Aristotle's *phronesis*, practical wisdom, was calibrated to the polis. A wise person could reason about
the consequences of their actions on the people around them: their household, their friends, their city.
The scope of action and the scope of judgment roughly matched.

Technology has scaled far beyond that. Our tools can now affect the future of the entire human race, and
much of the non-human world with it. That is beyond the scale of any one individual to reason over. You
cannot cultivate a habit of good judgment about consequences you cannot perceive, spread across billions
of people and generations not yet born.

This is the territory Hans Jonas mapped in *The Imperative of Responsibility*: traditional ethics was
built for a world where the range of human action was small and its effects were close in space and time.
Jonas answered with a new imperative of responsibility. My question is different: if the scale of action
is now planetary, what would the corresponding *virtues* be? Planetary scale demands planetary virtues.

There is a second problem, and it is a problem of speed.

Virtue ethics depends on moral exemplars: people who have lived a good life and can show the rest of us
what it looks like. But accelerating novelty means we have no exemplars of what a good life with
accelerating technology actually looks like. No one has lived one yet. By the time anyone could, the
technology has changed again.

So virtue ethics is squeezed from both sides. The *scale* of technology outruns individual practical
wisdom, and the *speed* of technology outruns the exemplars that practical wisdom is learned from.

Open questions:

- What would a planetary virtue be? Is it a disposition of individuals, of institutions, or of something
  in between?
- Can exemplars be replaced by something else: simulations, stories, communities that run ahead of the
  rest of us?
- Is "adaptability" itself the virtue, as I suggested in
  [What Does a Good Digital Life Look Like?](/essays/what-does-a-good-digital-life-look), or is that a
  dodge?
