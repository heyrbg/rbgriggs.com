# Ideas in "Life is Special Enough"

An argument against "secret sauce" theories of human exceptionalism, replacing them with a "contingency argument" grounded in the rarity of life. It holds 7 distinct ideas, including an original reframe of human suboptimality as a strength.

## The contingency argument for human specialness
- **Type:** theory
- **Claim:** Griggs proposes that humans are special not because of any essential quality but because they exist as the contingent product of life, which as far as we know has happened only once, making life the most precious thing in the universe.
- **In the essay:** The central section "The contingency argument", fully developed. Anchor: "What makes us special is the simple fact that we're here."
- **Stands alone because:** It offers a clear alternative answer to "what is the point of humans if AI can do everything?" that does not depend on any capacity AI might match.
- **Micro-article:** "The Contingency Argument: Why Humans Matter in an Age of AI" · "If AI surpasses human abilities, what makes humans special?" · (1) The failure of essence-based answers; (2) Contingency: life could have not happened, and has happened once; (3) Value grounded in lineage to life, not in a unique faculty.
- **Prior art to engage:** Stephen Jay Gould's *Wonderful Life* (contingency of evolution); Rare Earth hypothesis; existentialist anti-essentialism (Sartre); Richard Rorty on contingency. Not cited in the essay.
- **Connections:** manifesto (reuses the "happened only once" argument nearly verbatim), the-reverse-turing-test, the-plurality-a-better-myth-for-ai
- **Strength:** 3

## Secret-sauce arguments keep losing because they look for an essence
- **Type:** critique
- **Claim:** Every historical claim to human uniqueness (God's creation, soul, cosmic center, reason, consciousness, language, free will, meaning-making) has been eroded by science or technology, and Griggs argues the deeper problem is the search for an essential quality at all.
- **In the essay:** The opening list and the "secret sauce" section, which surveys religious, scientific (quantum/analog/carbon-based consciousness), and free-will arguments. Anchor: "But what if no such essential quality exists?"
- **Stands alone because:** It names and diagnoses a recurring move in AI debates ("but machines can't really X"), and explains why that move is fragile.
- **Micro-article:** "Why 'Machines Can't Do X' Arguments Keep Failing" · "Is there anything humans can do that AI never will?" · (1) The shrinking list of human uniqueness; (2) The three modern secret-sauce families and their weaknesses; (3) Giving up essence as relief, not defeat.
- **Prior art to engage:** Freud's three blows to human narcissism (Copernicus, Darwin, Freud); Luciano Floridi's "fourth revolution"; Penrose-Hameroff quantum consciousness; John Searle's biological naturalism. Not cited in the essay.
- **Connections:** the-reverse-turing-test, moral-natures-of-humans-and-machines, manifesto
- **Strength:** 3

## Human capabilities are optimally human because they are suboptimal
- **Type:** reframe
- **Claim:** Griggs argues that human abilities are contingent adaptations to Earth, imperfect by design, and that their suboptimality (learning by failure, errors turned into serendipity, walks and naps, irrationality feeding imagination) is what makes them distinctly human.
- **In the essay:** A compact paragraph within the contingency section. Anchor: "It is this suboptimality that makes our capabilities so optimally human."
- **Stands alone because:** It inverts the optimization framing of AI comparisons and is memorable and quotable.
- **Micro-article:** "The Case for Human Suboptimality" · "Why are humans creative if we're less efficient than machines?" · (1) Capacities tuned to Earth, not to an ideal; (2) Errors, idleness, and irrationality as engines of insight; (3) Why optimizing humans toward machines loses what matters.
- **Prior art to engage:** Herbert Simon's bounded rationality; research on incubation and mind-wandering; Gerd Gigerenzer on heuristics. Not cited in the essay.
- **Connections:** the-reverse-turing-test, the-plurality-a-better-myth-for-ai, manifesto ("flawed machines meant to be optimized"), whats-the-deal-with-cognitive-augmentation
- **Strength:** 3

## Finitude is the engine of meaning
- **Type:** argument
- **Claim:** Griggs holds that our finitude (suffering, death, falling short of aspiration) drives transcendence, the quest for meaning, and the push to expand possibility, and that our most valued capacities (judgment, aesthetics, morals) arise from the clash of finitude and transcendence.
- **In the essay:** Two paragraphs in the contingency section. Anchor: "Yet this very finitude is what fuels everything that makes us human"
- **Stands alone because:** It explains why removing human limits (digital immortality, perfected skills) might not enhance what we value, a live question in transhumanism debates.
- **Micro-article:** "Why Finitude Makes Human Values Possible" · "Would humans lose meaning if technology removed our limits?" · (1) Finitude and transcendence in tension; (2) Judgment, aesthetics, morality as products of that tension; (3) Implications for transhumanist promises.
- **Prior art to engage:** Heidegger's being-toward-death; Martin Hägglund's *This Life*; Bernard Williams on immortality's tedium. Not cited in the essay.
- **Connections:** homo-digitalis, manifesto (digital immortality critique)
- **Strength:** 2

## Human values cannot be detached from the wholeness of life
- **Type:** argument
- **Claim:** Judgments, aesthetics, and moral values are not separable attributes but expressions of life's integrated wholeness, so they cannot be optimized or reproduced by isolating a capability; life is not a machine, computation, or algorithm.
- **In the essay:** Late in the contingency section. Anchor: "There is an integrated wholeness to life that imparts an intentionality onto every human capability that can never be captured by that capability alone."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a holism argument against capability-by-capability benchmarking of humans versus AI.
- **Micro-article:** "Why You Can't Benchmark a Human" · "Can AI replicate human judgment one skill at a time?" · (1) The decomposition assumption in AI comparisons; (2) Intentionality from wholeness; (3) What this means for evaluating AI.
- **Prior art to engage:** Hubert Dreyfus's *What Computers Can't Do*; enactivism (Varela, Thompson); Robert Rosen's *Life Itself*. Not cited in the essay.
- **Connections:** manifesto ("you are not a machine"), the-reverse-turing-test, infinite-dimensionality, the-high-dimensional-society
- **Strength:** 2

## Forgetting our contingency is what makes us cede agency to machines
- **Type:** argument
- **Claim:** Griggs argues that rejecting our lineage to life tempts us to remake humans as quantified and self-made, which leads us to use machines as the sole yardstick of human achievement and to cede agency to systems that cannot generate human values.
- **In the essay:** Penultimate paragraph. Anchor: "When we forget our contingency we forget our humanity."
- **Stands alone because:** It locates a cultural cause for machine-centric thinking, not just a technical one.
- **Micro-article:** "Why We Measure Ourselves Against Machines" · "Why do people compare human worth to AI capabilities?" · (1) The self-made human ideal; (2) Machines as the default comparison; (3) Recovering contingency as a corrective.
- **Prior art to engage:** Günther Anders's "Promethean shame"; Michael Sandel's *The Case Against Perfection*. Not cited in the essay.
- **Connections:** manifesto, the-reverse-turing-test
- **Strength:** 2

## Why humans want to be special
- **Type:** concept
- **Claim:** Griggs notes that human exceptionalism has functional payoffs: it builds in-group cohesion and shared identity, justifies dominance over nature, and eases existential terror about mortality.
- **In the essay:** Brief paragraph after the opening list. Anchor: "It reduces some of the existential terror that comes with our impermanence and mortality."
- **Stands alone because:** It explains why AI threatens people psychologically, not just economically, and why exceptionalism claims persist despite evidence.
- **Micro-article:** "Why AI Feels Like a Threat to Human Identity" · "Why do people feel threatened when AI matches human abilities?" · (1) Three functions of exceptionalism; (2) Technology as a history of dismantling it; (3) A contingent alternative that keeps meaning without ego.
- **Prior art to engage:** Terror management theory (Becker, Solomon, Greenberg, Pyszczynski); Freud's blows to narcissism. Not cited in the essay.
- **Connections:** the-reverse-turing-test, moral-natures-of-humans-and-machines
- **Strength:** 1
