# Ideas in "Towards a Philosophy of Technology"

A short "garage door open" post of ten numbered working notes, each a one-to-two-sentence thesis. All ten are distinct ideas, and most are seeds that other essays develop.

## Technology is philosophy made real
- **Type:** theory
- **Claim:** Griggs holds that every innovator works from some version of "the good" and uses technology to make it real, so every technology embodies a philosophy.
- **In the essay:** Note 1, two sentences, and the founding thesis of the Tech for Life project. Anchor: "Technology is philosophy made real."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a short, quotable thesis that answers "why does philosophy matter to technology?"
- **Micro-article:** "Technology Is Philosophy Made Real" · "Do technologies have values built into them?" ·
  - Every innovation starts from an implicit idea of the good
  - Building it puts that idea into the world at scale
  - So judging technology means judging the philosophy it carries
- **Prior art to engage:** Langdon Winner, "Do Artifacts Have Politics?"; value-sensitive design; Heidegger (none cited in the essay)
- **Connections:** how-philosophy-makes-technology-better, calling-all-philosophers, manifesto, the-question-concerning-digital-technology
- **Strength:** 3

## Technology and ecology are two sides of one planetary face
- **Type:** reframe
- **Claim:** Griggs holds that technology and ecology have merged, so every technological problem is turning into a planetary problem.
- **In the essay:** Note 2, one sentence. Anchor: "technology and ecology have become two sides of the same planetary face"
- **Stands alone because:** It names the shift from local to planetary stakes in a single image.
- **Micro-article:** "Why Every Technology Problem Is Now a Planetary Problem" · "How are technology and the environment connected?" ·
  - Technological effects now spread to planetary scale
  - Ecology can no longer be treated as outside technology
  - What follows for governance and design
- **Prior art to engage:** Benjamin Bratton, *The Terraforming* and "planetary computation"; Anthropocene debates (not cited in the essay)
- **Connections:** our-planetary-predicament, a-constraint-theory-of-technology (the planet as the most fundamental constraint)
- **Strength:** 2

## The bottleneck is coordination, not technology
- **Type:** argument
- **Claim:** Griggs holds that what limits our ability to solve planetary challenges is our inability to coordinate, not a lack of technology.
- **In the essay:** Note 3, one sentence. Anchor: "The limiting factor in solving planetary challenges is not technological limitation but our incapacity to coordinate."
- **Stands alone because:** It cuts against techno-solutionism directly, and it is easy to cite.
- **Micro-article:** "The Real Bottleneck Is Coordination" · "Why can't technology alone solve climate change and other global problems?" ·
  - We often have the technical means already
  - Collective action and coordination fail at planetary scale
  - Innovation should therefore go into coordination itself
- **Prior art to engage:** Elinor Ostrom; Scott Alexander's "Meditations on Moloch"; Daniel Schmachtenberger's coordination-failure framing (none cited in the essay)
- **Connections:** our-planetary-predicament, the-plurality-a-better-myth-for-ai, the-high-dimensional-society, manifesto
- **Strength:** 3

## Nothing beyond capitalism is big enough to guide technology
- **Type:** critique
- **Claim:** Griggs observes that democracy as it now exists seems less and less able to produce anything beyond capitalism large enough to act as technology's guide and judge.
- **In the essay:** Note 4, one sentence; developed fully in "A Constraint Theory of Technology" as "the market is not big enough." Anchor: "Democracy in its current forms seems increasingly less capable of generating something beyond capitalism that is big enough to become technology's guide and judge."
- **Stands alone because:** It frames the governance gap as a problem of scale and authority, not of regulatory detail.
- **Micro-article:** "Who Judges Technology When the Market Is the Only Judge?" · "What should guide technological development besides the market?" ·
  - The market has become technology's main guide by default
  - Democracy struggles to produce a rival
  - What a guide big enough would need
- **Prior art to engage:** Habermas on technocracy; Shoshana Zuboff (not cited in the essay)
- **Connections:** a-constraint-theory-of-technology, the-price-of-innovation, manifesto
- **Strength:** 2

## Advanced technology breaks evolution's trial-and-error
- **Type:** argument
- **Claim:** Griggs holds that evolution, as a model of adaptation, matters less and less because trial and error stops working as a way to adapt advanced technology, so new models are needed.
- **In the essay:** Note 5, one sentence; developed in other essays. Anchor: "We need new paradigms."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a recurring Griggs thesis with clear relevance to AI risk.
- **Micro-article:** see the cross-essay piece "Advanced Technology Breaks Trial and Error" (anchored in a-constraint-theory-of-technology) · "Why can't we just learn from mistakes with AI?" ·
  - Trial and error needs errors that are survivable, quick to show up and reversible
  - Advanced technology produces errors that are none of these
  - New models: safe-to-fail probes and constraint design
- **Prior art to engage:** Popper's piecemeal engineering; Nassim Taleb on ruin; the Collingridge dilemma (none cited in the essay)
- **Connections:** a-constraint-theory-of-technology, our-planetary-predicament, how-philosophy-makes-technology-better, manifesto, moral-natures-of-humans-and-machines, the-majesty-of-language
- **Strength:** 2

## The sci-fi imagination gap
- **Type:** prediction
- **Claim:** Griggs notes that science fiction writers seem unable to imagine a workable future that combines advanced technology with a flourishing humanity, and asks whether that is because such a future is impossible.
- **In the essay:** Note 6, posed as a provocation, not asserted. Anchor: "What if such a future is unimaginable because it is in fact impossible?"
- **Stands alone because:** It is a sharp, debatable question that ties culture to forecasting.
- **Micro-article:** "Why Can't Sci-Fi Imagine a Good Future?" · "Why is most science fiction dystopian?" ·
  - The pattern: advanced technology and human flourishing rarely appear together
  - Two readings: a failure of imagination, or a real impossibility
  - What it would take to imagine otherwise (protopias, solarpunk)
- **Prior art to engage:** Kevin Kelly's "protopia"; Neal Stephenson's Project Hieroglyph; solarpunk (not cited in the essay)
- **Connections:** how-philosophy-makes-technology-better, our-planetary-predicament
- **Strength:** 2

## Acceleration defies emergence
- **Type:** theory
- **Claim:** Griggs holds that technological change is now so fast that it outruns the carrying capacity of earlier hierarchical layers and keeps new thermodynamically stable equilibria from forming, which blocks emergence.
- **In the essay:** Note 7, one dense sentence. Anchor: "The accelerating rate of technological change defies emergence by exceeding the carrying capacity of previous hierarchical substrates"
- **Stands alone because:** It is an unusual complexity-science claim about why fast change destabilizes rather than renews; it is undeveloped and needs elaboration.
- **Micro-article:** "Too Fast to Emerge: Why Acceleration Prevents New Order" · "Can technological change happen too fast for society to adapt?" ·
  - Emergence needs stable substrates and time to settle into equilibrium
  - Acceleration exceeds what earlier layers can carry
  - Result: no new stable order forms
- **Prior art to engage:** Ilya Prigogine on dissipative structures; Herbert Simon's hierarchy of nearly decomposable systems; Alvin Toffler's *Future Shock*; Hartmut Rosa (none cited in the essay)
- **Connections:** manifesto (emergence), the-high-dimensional-society
- **Strength:** 2

## A new myth of technology in service of life
- **Type:** reframe
- **Claim:** Griggs holds that we need a new myth of technology that places the human between nature and technology and puts all three in service of life itself, and that the e/acc aim of increasing the universe's conversion of free energy into entropy is not enough.
- **In the essay:** Note 8, linking to Beff Jezos's e/acc principles as the foil. Anchor: "We need a new myth of technology that rightly situates the human between nature and technology and puts all three in service of life itself."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a positioning statement against e/acc, and it previews Griggs's larger mythic project.
- **Micro-article:** "Beyond Entropy: Why Technology Needs a Myth of Life" · "What's wrong with effective accelerationism's view of technology?" ·
  - e/acc's thermodynamic justification
  - Why maximizing entropy isn't a purpose
  - Human, nature and technology together in service of life
- **Prior art to engage:** e/acc (linked); Lewis Mumford's myth of the machine; Teilhard de Chardin (not cited in the essay)
- **Connections:** the-plurality-a-better-myth-for-ai, life-is-special-enough, manifesto, progress-towards-what
- **Strength:** 2

## All technological questions converge on the religious
- **Type:** prediction
- **Claim:** Griggs holds that once technology passes a minimal threshold of advancement, its questions become religious ones: what, if anything, is sacred about the human, whether anything should limit the pursuit of immortality, and what technology is even for.
- **In the essay:** Note 9, with three example questions. Anchor: "Upon reaching a minimally advanced threshold, all technological questions converge on the religious."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a bold, citable thesis about how secular technology ends up asking sacred questions.
- **Micro-article:** "Why Advanced Technology Raises Religious Questions" · "Is AI a religious question?" ·
  - The threshold where technical questions become questions about ultimate purpose
  - Examples: what is sacred about the human, limits on immortality, what technology is for
  - Implication: secular frameworks run out
- **Prior art to engage:** David Noble, *The Religion of Technology*; Jacques Ellul; Tillich's "ultimate concern" (none cited here)
- **Connections:** the-case-for-immaterial-progress (ultimate concerns), life-is-special-enough, how-philosophy-makes-technology-better, the-question-concerning-digital-technology
- **Strength:** 3

## The machine's gaze will force us to resolve the death of God
- **Type:** prediction
- **Claim:** Griggs proposes, as a paradox, that only technology can save us, because confronting the "dead gaze" of the machine will force humanity to finally resolve the death of God together.
- **In the essay:** Note 10, two sentences. Anchor: "The dead gaze of the machine staring back at us will become the forcing function we need to collectively resolve" (the phrase continues with a link on "the death of god")
- **Stands alone because:** It is a provocative reading of AI as a cultural forcing function for answering Nietzsche's challenge.
- **Micro-article:** "AI and the Death of God: Technology as a Forcing Function" · "What does AI have to do with Nietzsche's death of God?" ·
  - Nietzsche's challenge left unresolved
  - The machine as a mirror that shows the void
  - Why the confrontation may force a collective answer
- **Prior art to engage:** Nietzsche (linked); Heidegger's "only a god can save us" (echoed but not cited)
- **Connections:** how-philosophy-makes-technology-better (Enlightenment values filling the void left by the death of God), the-reverse-turing-test, moral-natures-of-humans-and-machines
- **Strength:** 2
