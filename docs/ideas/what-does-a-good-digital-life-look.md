# Ideas in "What Does a Good Digital Life Look Like?"

Part two of the digital-reality series, framed as advice to a thirteen-year-old digital native. It develops one strong original theory (a four-stage model of how the rate of cultural change breaks intergenerational wisdom) plus 5 supporting ideas, for 6 in total.

## Four stages of cultural change and the transmission of wisdom
- **Type:** theory
- **Claim:** Griggs proposes that the transfer of values across generations depends on the rate of cultural change, moving through four stages (meta-generational, multi-generational, inter-generational, intra-generational), with traditional ethical frameworks breaking down as change accelerates.
- **In the essay:** The backbone of the essay, with a section per stage. Anchor: "is there a rate of change where it begins to break down completely?"
- **Stands alone because:** It is a crisp, original typology that can be applied to AI, parenting, education, and moral philosophy without the digital-native frame.
- **Micro-article:** "The Four Speeds of Cultural Change" · "Why can't parents give useful advice about technology to their kids?" · (1) Meta- and multi-generational: stable contexts and elders as bridges; (2) Inter-generational: the chasm (WWI Europe, 1960s America, Cultural Revolution, digital); (3) Intra-generational: change within a single lifetime.
- **Prior art to engage:** Margaret Mead's *Culture and Commitment* (postfigurative, cofigurative, prefigurative cultures, a close parallel); Alvin Toffler's *Future Shock*; Karl Mannheim on generations. Not cited in the essay.
- **Connections:** the-question-concerning-digital-technology (rate-of-change problem for analysis), homo-digitalis
- **Strength:** 3

## The contextual chasm
- **Type:** concept
- **Claim:** When contexts shift abruptly, traditional ideas of a good life stop applying not because they are false but because the context that made those lives possible no longer exists, producing a chasm across which wisdom cannot transfer.
- **In the essay:** Introduced in the opening thought experiment and defined in Stage 3. Anchor: "not because they are no longer true, but because the context in which those lives were possible no longer exists."
- **Stands alone because:** It names a specific, distinct cause of the generation gap (context loss, not value disagreement) and is a coinable term.
- **Micro-article:** "The Contextual Chasm: Why Old Wisdom Stops Working" · "Why does traditional advice feel irrelevant to digital natives?" · (1) Values versus contexts; (2) Translation failure in practice; (3) Why rebellion replaces veneration.
- **Prior art to engage:** Alasdair MacIntyre's *After Virtue* (fragmented moral traditions); Jonathan Haidt's *The Anxious Generation* (the essay links to After Babel). Mead not cited.
- **Connections:** homo-digitalis
- **Strength:** 3

## Intra-generational change as the new normal
- **Type:** prediction
- **Claim:** Griggs predicts that with AI, robotics, and bio-engineering, change will increasingly happen within a single lifetime, so lessons learned early stop applying later and adopter/non-adopter chasms open (for example the first "AI natives" closer to AI nannies than family).
- **In the essay:** "The new normal" section. Anchor: "it creates friction during a single lifetime."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a falsifiable forward-looking claim about AI's effect on moral life and childhood.
- **Micro-article:** "When Wisdom Expires Within a Lifetime" · "How will AI change how we pass on values to children?" · (1) Defining intra-generational change; (2) AI natives and AI companions; (3) Why a return to equilibrium is unlikely.
- **Prior art to engage:** Toffler's future shock; Ray Kurzweil's accelerating returns; research on teen AI companion use (the essay cites The Verge). Toffler and Kurzweil not cited.
- **Connections:** homo-digitalis, whats-the-deal-with-cognitive-augmentation
- **Strength:** 2

## Virtue ethics depends on stable contexts
- **Type:** critique
- **Claim:** Moral habituation and moral exemplars, the Aristotelian method used across historic cultures, work only when contexts stay largely the same between generations, so their efficiency declines as change accelerates.
- **In the essay:** "How to define a good life" and Stage 1. Anchor: "This type of moral education depended on contexts between generations remaining largely the same."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a concise critique of applying virtue ethics naively to fast-changing tech environments.
- **Micro-article:** "Can Virtue Ethics Survive Accelerating Change?" · "Does Aristotle's virtue ethics still work in a digital world?" · (1) Habituation and exemplars; (2) The hidden stability assumption; (3) What must change: dynamic role models and wisdom.
- **Prior art to engage:** Aristotle (cited); Shannon Vallor's *Technology and the Virtues* (technomoral virtues, a close parallel); MacIntyre. Vallor not cited.
- **Connections:** how-philosophy-makes-technology-better
- **Strength:** 2

## Five principles for an ethics of change
- **Type:** design principle
- **Claim:** Griggs proposes that ethics resilient to rapid contextual shifts should abstract core values, venerate adaptability as a virtue, teach ethical reasoning from first principles, make understanding the essence of technology part of moral education, and restore intergenerational dialogue.
- **In the essay:** Numbered list in "A new ethics of change", plus a caveat against relativism. Anchor: "The ability to navigate change itself should be recognized as a crucial virtue"
- **Stands alone because:** It is a practical, list-shaped framework that answers "how do we teach ethics for an AI future?"
- **Micro-article:** "Five Principles for Ethics in an Age of Accelerating Change" · "How should we teach kids ethics when technology keeps changing?" · (1) Abstract values, adaptability as virtue; (2) First-principles reasoning and technological awareness; (3) Dialogue across generations, without relativism.
- **Prior art to engage:** Vallor's technomoral wisdom; Nassim Taleb's antifragility; Mead's prefigurative learning. Not cited in the essay.
- **Connections:** the-question-concerning-digital-technology (essence of technology), manifesto (adaptation principle), the-plurality-a-better-myth-for-ai
- **Strength:** 2

## A good life as fidelity to ideals, not a fixed ideal
- **Type:** reframe
- **Claim:** Griggs suggests the good digital life is not about reaching a fixed ideal but about being true to one's ideals at each step of a changing journey.
- **In the essay:** The closing italic advice to the thirteen-year-old. Anchor: "the pursuit of a good life is not about achieving some fixed ideal"
- **Stands alone because:** It is a quotable reframe of eudaimonia for a world without stable contexts.
- **Micro-article:** "What Should We Tell a Teenager About Living Well Online?" · "What advice should I give my kid about living a good life with technology?" · (1) Why "stay off your phone" fails; (2) Discernment amid noise; (3) Flexibility without losing core principles.
- **Prior art to engage:** Aristotle's eudaimonia; existentialist authenticity. Not cited beyond Aristotle.
- **Connections:** homo-digitalis
- **Strength:** 1
