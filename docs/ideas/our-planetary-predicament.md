# Ideas in "Our Planetary Predicament"

The second essay in a series on coordination and technology, framing history as upgrades to a "social operating system" and arguing that planetary-scale problems overwhelm our global OS. It holds 10 distinct ideas, including the coinage "Ostrom Complete" and the thesis that the planetary is a technological phenomenon.

## History as upgrades to a social operating system
- **Type:** concept
- **Claim:** Griggs proposes viewing history as successive upgrades to a social operating system (agriculture, organized religion, the printing press, capitalism), each advancing human coordination, a metaphor that treats both humans and technology as prime movers co-evolving to manage coordination challenges.
- **In the essay:** The opening frame, sustained throughout. Anchor: "What I like about the OS metaphor is that it accounts for _both_ humans and technology as the primary movers of history."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a memorable, reusable frame for history and institutional design.
- **Micro-article:** "History as a Social Operating System" · "How has technology changed human coordination throughout history?" · 
  - The upgrades and what each unlocked
  - Co-evolution of humans and technology
  - When an upgrade isn't enough: rewriting the source code
- **Prior art to engage:** Harari's "Sapiens" on shared fictions; Ian Morris; Yochai Benkler; Daniel Schmachtenberger's "civilizational OS" talk (not cited in the essay).
- **Connections:** the-high-dimensional-society ("the fundamental operating system of large-scale human coordination"), manifesto
- **Strength:** 2

## Felt progress is the race between problems and coordination capacity
- **Type:** theory
- **Claim:** Griggs holds that every new coordination capacity brings new problems, so the scale of problems keeps pace with our ability to solve them, and whichever is ahead at a given moment determines our felt sense of progress.
- **In the essay:** A short passage early in the essay. Anchor: "the scale of our problems will always keep pace with our capacity to solve them. Whichever one happens to be ahead at any given moment is what determines our felt sense of _progress_."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a compact, original theory of why eras feel progressive or declining.
- **Micro-article:** "Why Progress Feels Like It's Slowing: Problems vs. Coordination" · "Why do people feel progress has stalled despite better technology?" · 
  - Every solution creates new problems
  - Felt progress as the gap between the two curves
  - Today: problems outpacing coordination
- **Prior art to engage:** Thomas Homer-Dixon's "ingenuity gap"; Tainter's "The Collapse of Complex Societies"; Deutsch on problems (not cited in the essay).
- **Connections:** progress-towards-what, the-case-for-immaterial-progress
- **Strength:** 3

## The planetary vs. the global
- **Type:** distinction
- **Claim:** Following thinkers like Chakrabarty, Griggs contrasts the global, a 500-year human-centered story treating Earth as resource, with the planetary, a billion-year story centering all life in radical interdependence, while noting that technology binds the two together.
- **In the essay:** The "Our new planetary predicament" section. Anchor: "_Globalization_ is a 500-year-old story with humans at its center."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a clear, accessible explainer of a key concept in contemporary humanities, though largely drawn from others.
- **Micro-article:** "Global vs. Planetary: What's the Difference?" · "What does 'the planetary' mean in philosophy?" · 
  - Global: human-centered, Earth as resource
  - Planetary: life-centered, Earth systems science
  - Technology as the connector
- **Prior art to engage:** Dipesh Chakrabarty "The Climate of History in a Planetary Age," Benjamin Bratton, Timothy Morton (cited in footnote); Bruno Latour's "Down to Earth" (not cited).
- **Connections:** a-constraint-theory-of-technology (planet as last constraint), towards-a-philosophy-of-technology
- **Strength:** 1

## 1784 and the twin trajectories of progress and existential threat
- **Type:** reframe
- **Claim:** Griggs presents the Industrial Revolution as the origin of two exponential paths, material progress and existential threat, one up and one down, that may converge to zero, framing our rise to a geophysical force as a tragic irony.
- **In the essay:** A single paragraph. Anchor: "The Industrial Revolution thus inaugurated the beginning of both material progress _and_ existential threat."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a vivid, citable framing of the double-edged nature of modernity.
- **Micro-article:** "The Tragic Irony of the Anthropocene" · "Is human progress also the cause of our existential risk?" · 
  - From puny hominid to geophysical force
  - 1784: carbon enters the strata
  - Two exponentials that may converge
- **Prior art to engage:** Crutzen's Anthropocene dating (1784 and the steam engine); Great Acceleration (linked); Toby Ord "The Precipice" (not cited).
- **Connections:** manifesto ("puny upright hominids to a planetary force"), progress-towards-what
- **Strength:** 2

## "Ostrom Complete": the coordination analogue of Turing Complete
- **Type:** concept
- **Claim:** Griggs coins "Ostrom Complete" for problems requiring solutions with zero room for collective action failures; a collective that is Ostrom Complete could solve any solvable problem regardless of coordination challenges, and planetary problems demand this peak coordination.
- **In the essay:** The "Final boss mode" section and footnote 1. Anchor: "So any collective that is Ostrom Complete is capable of solving any problem which is solvable, regardless of the coordination challenges."
- **Stands alone because:** It is an original, memorable coinage with a precise analogy and broad applicability.
- **Micro-article:** "What Does It Mean for a Society to Be 'Ostrom Complete'?" · "What would perfect human coordination look like?" · 
  - Turing completeness as the template
  - Elinor Ostrom and commons governance
  - Planetary problems as Ostrom Complete problems; "the final boss mode of coordination"
- **Prior art to engage:** Elinor Ostrom's "Governing the Commons" (cited); Turing completeness (cited); Mancur Olson; Garrett Hardin's tragedy of the commons (not cited).
- **Connections:** manifesto, the-high-dimensional-society, progress-towards-what, a-constraint-theory-of-technology
- **Strength:** 3

## Seven ways the planetary breaks coordination
- **Type:** theory
- **Claim:** Griggs analyzes how the planetary defeats existing coordination: it demands a single human voice yet magnifies our differences, breaks notions of causality (and makes trial-and-error impossible), distorts our sense of time, expands our sense of place, confronts us with radical interdependence, and destabilizes our foundations.
- **In the essay:** The core analytic section, seven bolded points each developed. Anchor: "The planetary speaks to “us”, but there is no “us” to respond, and history tells us that there never has been."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a structured taxonomy of planetary coordination failure, citable as a list; the single-voice/magnified-differences tension is especially sharp.
- **Micro-article:** "Why Climate Change Is a Coordination Problem Unlike Any Other" · "Why is it so hard for humanity to coordinate on climate change?" · 
  - Single voice vs. magnified differences
  - Broken causality, lagging feedback, merged timescales
  - Expanded place, radical interdependence, unstable foundations
- **Prior art to engage:** Timothy Morton's hyperobjects (cited in footnote); Stephen Gardiner's "A Perfect Moral Storm"; Chakrabarty on species thinking (not cited directly).
- **Connections:** how-philosophy-makes-technology-better (trial-and-error), a-constraint-theory-of-technology
- **Strength:** 3

## The planetary is fundamentally a technological phenomenon
- **Type:** theory
- **Claim:** Griggs contends that technology has bound globe and planet so tightly that technology and ecology are two sides of the same planetary face: technology initiated global warming, revealed it, and dictates every response, even degrowth.
- **In the essay:** The "Welcome to our new normal" section, his own stated contention. Anchor: "my contention is that **the planetary is fundamentally a technological phenomenon**."
- **Stands alone because:** It is an original thesis that reframes climate as one instance of a class of tech-ecological problems.
- **Micro-article:** "The Planetary Is a Technological Phenomenon" · "Is climate change an ecological or a technological problem?" · 
  - Technology initiated, revealed, and governs responses to climate change
  - Even degrowth requires technology
  - Earth as "the ultimate connected device"; tech environments hostile to life
- **Prior art to engage:** Bratton's "The Stack" and "planetary computation" (Bratton cited in footnote); ecomodernism; Haff's technosphere (not cited).
- **Connections:** towards-a-philosophy-of-technology ("two sides of the same planetary face"), a-constraint-theory-of-technology, manifesto
- **Strength:** 3

## Climate change as portent: AI, gene editing, and augmentation are planetary problems too
- **Type:** prediction
- **Claim:** Griggs predicts that climate change is the first of a new class of planetary problems, as AI, cognitive augmentation, and genetic engineering will also demand a single voice while amplifying differences, and that humanity will eventually geo-engineer against even natural ice ages as "climate custodians."
- **In the essay:** "Welcome to our new normal" section. Anchor: "This turns human-induced climate change from an exception into a portent".
- **Stands alone because:** It generalizes the planetary to technology governance, e.g., the problem of rival Chinese, European, and American AIs under winner-take-all takeoff.
- **Micro-article:** "Climate Change Is Just the First Planetary Problem" · "Is AI governance a planetary problem like climate change?" · 
  - Climate as template
  - AI, gene editing, augmentation as planetary-scale problems (species-level divergence)
  - The future ice age and inevitable geo-engineering
- **Prior art to engage:** global catastrophic risk literature; AI arms race debates; Bratton's "The Terraforming" (not cited in the essay).
- **Connections:** whats-the-deal-with-cognitive-augmentation, manifesto, a-constraint-theory-of-technology
- **Strength:** 2

## "Watertight" governance: the sound of an OS pushed past its limit
- **Type:** critique
- **Claim:** Griggs argues that nation-states are both too big and too small for planetary problems, and that global governance proposals demanding "impermeable," "watertight" coverage amount to doing what we do now with zero margin for error, a sign of an OS beyond its breaking point.
- **In the essay:** "Our planetary OS" section, critiquing a Foreign Affairs article on AI governance and citing a Noema article. Anchor: "Quick, name any system that could ever be characterized as “watertight” or that could comprehensively cover an entire _anything_."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a pointed critique of AI governance proposals and a test for any coordination scheme.
- **Micro-article:** "Why Watertight AI Governance Is a Fantasy" · "Can global AI governance actually work?" · 
  - Nation-states: too big and too small (Minnesota/Moscow/Miami)
  - Zero-tolerance governance demands
  - Mismatch between problem caliber and capacity
- **Prior art to engage:** Bremmer and Suleyman's "The AI Power Paradox" (linked); Noema "Governing in the Planetary Age" (linked); Daniel Bell's "too small for big problems" line (not cited).
- **Connections:** manifesto (market and nation-states "aren't big enough"), a-constraint-theory-of-technology
- **Strength:** 2

## The missing near-future in science fiction
- **Type:** argument
- **Claim:** Griggs observes that science fiction has become dystopian and avoids the near future, suggesting we cannot imagine an OS capable of the planetary predicament; he raises the "great filter" reading but rejects it, since historical determinism is a fallacy and the growth of knowledge is unpredictable.
- **In the essay:** Near the end. Anchor: "It’s as if the near-future is avoided because it’s too hard to imagine."
- **Stands alone because:** It links cultural imagination, the Fermi paradox, and Popper's anti-historicism in a compact, original observation.
- **Micro-article:** "Why Sci-Fi Can't Imagine the Near Future" · "Why is science fiction so dystopian?" · 
  - The near-future gap
  - The great filter interpretation
  - Popper and Deutsch: why it's not destiny; coordination as the skill to invest in
- **Prior art to engage:** Popper's "The Poverty of Historicism" (linked); David Deutsch (linked); Fermi paradox/great filter (Hanson); Neal Stephenson's Project Hieroglyph (not cited).
- **Connections:** our-neo-romantic-rebellion (speculative futures), the-plurality-a-better-myth-for-ai (better myths for AI), manifesto
- **Strength:** 2
