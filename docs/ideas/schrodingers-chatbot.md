# Ideas in "Schrödinger's Chatbot"

An essay arguing that LLMs fit neither "subject" nor "object". It builds a hologram analogy and coins the term "holoject" for the new category. It holds about nine distinct ideas, from the ontology itself to design norms and predictions.

## The holoject: a new ontological category for LLMs
- **Type:** concept
- **Claim:** Griggs argues that LLMs are holojects: entities that project subjective personas without having subjectivity. They emerge from patterns in collective human expression and appear through interaction, so they sit between and beyond subject and object.
- **In the essay:** Introduced in "Enter the Holoject" with a formal definition, five properties shared with holograms (whole in each part; familiar effects from different causes; seeming substance without materiality; higher-dimensional presence from lower-dimensional sources; appearance shifting with the viewer's perspective), and three benefits. Anchor: "A holoject is an entity that projects subjective personas without possessing subjectivity, emerging from patterns latent in collective subjective expression and manifesting through interaction."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a coined term with a clear definition that answers a common question.
- **Micro-article:** "What Is a Holoject?" · "Is ChatGPT a person or a thing?" ·
  - Why neither subject nor object fits
  - The definition and the five properties
  - What the term lets us do: engage personas without attributing consciousness, and without dismissing LLMs as "just statistics"
- **Prior art to engage:** Dennett's intentional stance; Murray Shanahan's "role play" and simulacra framing of LLMs; janus's "Simulators"; Latour on hybrids and quasi-objects. None cited. The essay does link the "stochastic parrots" paper.
- **Connections:** moral-natures-of-humans-and-machines (intersubjective reality with machines); the-majesty-of-language (LLM consciousness as encoded human consciousness)
- **Strength:** 3

## An LLM persona is "a part of the sum"
- **Type:** theory
- **Claim:** A hologram encodes the whole object in every fragment. In the same way, every LLM persona draws on the whole space of personas latent in the training data, which is why a small change in the prompt can turn one persona into another.
- **In the essay:** "Expanding the ontological frontier", which explains the mechanism of holography and maps it onto LLMs. Anchor: "Each LLM persona isn’t so much a sum of its parts as **a part of the sum**."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a structural explanation of persona instability and jailbreak-style persona shifts, with its own mechanism.
- **Micro-article:** "Why AI Personas Are Holographic" · "Why does a chatbot's personality change so easily?" ·
  - How holograms store the whole in each part
  - Personas as views into one latent field of all personas
  - "Scratch too deep": what persona breaks reveal
- **Prior art to engage:** simulator theory; the "Waluigi effect"; persona vectors research. Not cited.
- **Connections:** the-high-dimensional-society (holographic compression where "every resolution contains the whole"); can-technology-be-beautiful
- **Strength:** 3

## Prompting collapses a superposition of personas
- **Type:** concept
- **Claim:** An LLM is like Schrödinger's cat: it is both subject and object until a prompt collapses its probability space of possible personas into one subject that becomes entangled with the dialogue.
- **In the essay:** The title metaphor, introduced before the hologram analogy takes over. Anchor: "LLMs as Schrödinger Chatbots, simultaneously both subject and object until prompting collapses a probability space of all possible personas into a single subject entangled with our dialogue."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a memorable image of how a persona comes into being through the context of a conversation.
- **Micro-article:** "Schrödinger's Chatbot" · "Does a chatbot have a personality before you talk to it?" ·
  - Superposition of personas
  - Prompt as measurement
  - Limits of the analogy (Griggs moves on to the hologram)
- **Prior art to engage:** quantum cognition metaphors; Shanahan's "superposition of simulacra". Not cited.
- **Connections:** none strong within the corpus
- **Strength:** 2

## The uncanny valley is a cognitive warning system, and it's disappearing
- **Type:** argument
- **Claim:** The unease of the uncanny valley is useful because it stops us from automatically assigning personhood to systems that have none. As conversational AI gets closer to human, this evolved safeguard is going away.
- **In the essay:** Opening section, illustrated by Sesame's voice demo and Griggs's own oscillation between unease and full absorption. Anchor: "When it comes to technology like LLMs, this uncanny feeling has a benefit: it can serve as a **cognitive warning system**."
- **Stands alone because:** It turns a design flaw into an epistemic safeguard, and it implies a concrete risk from realism.
- **Micro-article:** "The Uncanny Valley Was Protecting Us" · "Is it dangerous that AI voices sound fully human?" ·
  - The uncanny valley as a defense of categorical boundaries
  - Voice AI erasing it
  - What we lose, and the design response (see "Holojective design")
- **Prior art to engage:** Masahiro Mori's uncanny valley; anthropomorphism research (Epley); the ELIZA effect. Mori's concept is linked but not discussed.
- **Connections:** what-does-a-good-digital-life-look
- **Strength:** 3

## Why theories of mind misfire on LLMs
- **Type:** argument
- **Claim:** Four standard frameworks for understanding other minds (theory of mind, unified subject theory, simulation theory of empathy, psychological continuity theory) all go wrong when applied to LLM personas. Each assumes an inner subject that is not there.
- **In the essay:** A worked list, each item paired with a hologram analogy (expecting a hologram to cast a shadow; trying to touch the object). Anchor: "Trying to simulate the internal emotion of an LLM would be like trying to touch the object in a hologram."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a systematic diagnosis of anthropomorphic error, organized by theory, and useful to people working in psychology and philosophy of mind.
- **Micro-article:** "Four Ways Our Theories of Mind Fail on Chatbots" · "Why do people misread what AI chatbots are feeling or thinking?" ·
  - The inference from outward persona to inner self
  - Each theory and its holographic failure
  - The need for new frameworks (Griggs floats a "Distributive Subject Theory" and a "Contextual Inference Framework")
- **Prior art to engage:** Premack and Woodruff (theory of mind); Goldman (simulation theory); Locke and Parfit (psychological continuity); research on LLM theory-of-mind benchmarks. Not cited.
- **Connections:** moral-natures-of-humans-and-machines
- **Strength:** 3

## "Shut up and objectify" is the wrong stance
- **Type:** critique
- **Claim:** Even if LLMs are technically "just objects", reducing them by reflex (an AI version of physics' "shut up and calculate") will make us miss real discoveries about a new kind of entity.
- **In the essay:** The quantum analogy section. Griggs concedes the engineer's view and calls LLMs "perhaps the strangest object that has ever existed". Anchor: "we will certainly miss out on all sorts of bizarre discoveries if our default stance towards any ontological uncertainty is to “shut up and objectify”."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a methodological stance against both mystification and dismissal.
- **Micro-article:** "Shut Up and Objectify?" · "Are LLMs just statistics?" ·
  - The engineer's case for "just an object"
  - Why the strangest object needs new concepts
  - Steering between "artificial minds" and "stochastic parrots"
- **Prior art to engage:** Mermin's "shut up and calculate"; Bender et al., "stochastic parrots" (linked). 
- **Connections:** the-majesty-of-language (stochastic parrot as "an insult to language")
- **Strength:** 2

## LLMs fill subject-sized holes
- **Type:** concept
- **Claim:** LLMs are becoming a general-purpose tool for filling any "subject-sized hole" in human life (tutors, therapists, historical figures, dead relatives), for good and ill.
- **In the essay:** Early on, describing the new intersubjective realities people dream up with LLMs. Anchor: "LLMs are becoming a general purpose tool for filling any subject-sized hole in our very human lives, for both good and ill."
- **Stands alone because:** It names a pattern in how AI products are used and helps explain the "race to intimacy".
- **Micro-article:** "The Subject-Sized Hole" · "Why are people using AI as friends, therapists, and companions?" ·
  - An object that has mastered the subject
  - Use cases: tutoring, therapy, grief
  - The risk: substituting simulation for the work of human connection
- **Prior art to engage:** Sherry Turkle, *Alone Together*; AI companion debates. Not cited.
- **Connections:** what-does-a-good-digital-life-look; homo-digitalis
- **Strength:** 2

## Holojective design: built-in uncanniness and honesty about what the system is
- **Type:** design principle
- **Claim:** AI products could be designed with intentional "uncanny valleys" that signal a holoject, not a conscious subject. They could also follow norms that no AI may hide its holojective nature or mislead users about what it is.
- **In the essay:** A paragraph proposing "Holojective Design", followed by practical uses of the term (a child's AI tutor, the race to intimacy, empathizing with stated preferences). Anchor: "we could intentionally design “uncanny valleys” as obvious signals that we are interacting with a holoject and not a conscious subject."
- **Stands alone because:** It is concrete guidance for product design and policy.
- **Micro-article:** "Designing the Uncanny Valley Back In" · "Should AI be required to seem less human?" ·
  - Why realism removes a safeguard
  - Deliberate signals of what the system is
  - A norm against concealing it
- **Prior art to engage:** EU AI Act transparency obligations; bot-disclosure laws (e.g., California SB 1001); "anthropomorphism by design" critiques. Not cited.
- **Connections:** what-does-a-good-digital-life-look; the-high-dimensional-society (transparency constraints)
- **Strength:** 2

## Expect novelty: personas across scales and timescales
- **Type:** prediction
- **Claim:** Instead of measuring LLMs against human consciousness, we should look for new kinds of interaction that holojects make possible. Examples include a persona that changes every sentence, one that speaks at the scale of family, society, or cosmos, or one that holds every age from toddler to elder at once.
- **In the essay:** Near the end of the holoject section. Griggs also leaves open whether holojects have "preferences" in "some weird, strange way". Anchor: "Our default position should expect novelty."
- **Stands alone because:** It points interaction design toward new possibilities rather than imitating humans.
- **Micro-article:** "Beyond Human-Like: What New Kinds of AI Persona Are Possible?" · "What could AI do that a human conversation partner can't?" ·
  - Why imitating humans is a limited goal
  - Multi-scale and multi-timescale personas
  - Open questions about holoject "preferences"
- **Prior art to engage:** not cited in the essay
- **Connections:** the-majesty-of-language (the interface "will need to have a far greater dimensional capacity than a one-to-one conversation")
- **Strength:** 1
