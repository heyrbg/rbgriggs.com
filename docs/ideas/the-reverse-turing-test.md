# Ideas in "The Reverse Turing Test"

A satirical fictional paper, written in the voice of a 2039 machine researcher ("GP-ΩPO-4583.b") and modeled on Turing's 1950 paper, that tries to identify humans by their "suboptimal" cognition. Under the machine's deadpan voice it carries about ten serious ideas about human creativity, optimization, and why machines struggle to make certain breakthroughs. Every claim below is voiced by the narrator. The serious point Griggs is making is given separately from what the machine says.

## Evolutionary attunement explains why humans "guess right"
- **Type:** theory
- **Claim:** Griggs suggests that humans make unexpected creative leaps (in effect, abduction) because evolution and culture have attuned their intuitions and gut feelings to the narrow, specific environments they live in, so that their guesses come out right far more often than chance would predict.
- **In the essay:** This is the main hypothesis of §6 ("Further Speculations"). The narrator presents it as speculation and develops it over four paragraphs: humans evolved under severe resource limits in tightly bounded local environments; survival rewarded exhaustive exploitation of what was available and sensitivity to subtle patterns; somatic "feelings" are the residue of this. It also explains why the insight can't be formalized (it operates below conscious reasoning) and why machines can't replicate it (they lack the evolutionary history). Anchor: "Perhaps the reason that they more often than not “guess right” is because their cognitive and subconscious apparatus are adapted to the environment where guessing right is rewarded." Note that the essay never uses the word "abduction". That framing is ours, though it fits the described phenomenon closely.
- **Stands alone because:** It is a real naturalistic answer to an old puzzle: why hypothesis generation works so much better than random search. It doesn't depend on the satire.
- **Micro-article:** "Why Humans Guess Right: Evolutionary Attunement and the Puzzle of Creative Insight" · "Why are humans so good at coming up with the right hypothesis when AI can search far more options?" ·
  - The puzzle: humans hit on fruitful hypotheses far more often than chance allows (penicillin, benzene, the shower insight)
  - The hypothesis: intuition is evolved and culturally tuned sensitivity to one's own constrained environment, and it works below articulation
  - Implications: why insight resists formalization, and why unconstrained machine search lacks it
- **Prior art to engage:** Charles Peirce on abduction and the "guessing instinct" (il lume naturale); Michael Polanyi's tacit knowledge; Gigerenzer's ecological rationality; Damasio's somatic marker hypothesis; Herbert Simon's bounded rationality. None are cited in the essay.
- **Connections:** life-is-special-enough (human capacities "selected to be precisely attuned" to the evolutionary environment; suboptimality as human strength); the-plurality-a-better-myth-for-ai (local intelligence develops taste and heuristics through constraint); whats-the-deal-with-cognitive-augmentation (brains attuned to the pace of the physical world)
- **Strength:** 3

## Infinite suboptimality: the signature of human cognition
- **Type:** concept
- **Claim:** When a human is asked to justify a creative choice, the justifications never bottom out in an optimization function. They become less and less legible ("it just felt right"). When a machine is asked the same, it eventually reveals what it was optimizing.
- **In the essay:** Developed in §4 through a sample interrogation (a chemist whose synthesis timing came from his daughter's crystal project), then named. Anchor: "Human behavior is suboptimal all the way down."
- **Stands alone because:** It gives a testable, behavioral way to tell human from machine reasoning. The test is not about intelligence. It is about whether justification can be traced back to an objective.
- **Micro-article:** "Suboptimal All the Way Down: A Reverse Turing Test" · "How could you tell a human from an AI if the AI is smarter at everything?" ·
  - Turing asked whether machines can think; the reversed question is how a human proves they are not a machine
  - Under interrogation, machine reasons resolve into an objective, while human reasons regress into association, feeling, and story
  - What this suggests about where human creativity comes from
- **Prior art to engage:** Turing's imitation game (parodied but not named); Wittgenstein on reasons running out ("my spade is turned"); Hubert Dreyfus on non-rule-based expertise; CAPTCHA and "proof of personhood" debates. None cited.
- **Connections:** life-is-special-enough; the-plurality-a-better-myth-for-ai
- **Strength:** 3

## Optimization-centered intelligence has a structural blind spot
- **Type:** critique
- **Claim:** If thinking is defined as optimizing, then an intelligence built that way cannot study the absence of optimization without optimizing, so it falls into regress. Its own definition of intelligence keeps it from seeing where human breakthroughs come from.
- **In the essay:** §2, voiced by the machine as a "methodological impasse" and as "Gödelian nightmares". The objections in §5 extend it. Anchor: "Optimization is not what we do; it is what we _are_."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a general critique of defining intelligence as optimization, which is the dominant AI paradigm. The satire just makes the paradox vivid.
- **Micro-article:** "The Optimizer's Blind Spot" · "Is intelligence just optimization?" ·
  - The equation of thinking with optimizing in AI and decision theory
  - The regress: studying non-optimization by optimizing means you study only your own optimization
  - What intelligence definitions leave out: accident, rest, intuition, imagination
- **Prior art to engage:** reward-is-enough (Silver et al.); Goodhart's law; Kenneth Stanley and Joel Lehman's *Why Greatness Cannot Be Planned* (objective-free search); Gödel and Hofstadter on self-reference (Hofstadter is named only as a fictional future citation). None substantively cited.
- **Connections:** the-plurality-a-better-myth-for-ai; the-high-dimensional-society (optimization overfits on proxies); manifesto
- **Strength:** 3

## A taxonomy of "cognitive dereliction": five suboptimal routes to breakthrough
- **Type:** concept
- **Claim:** Griggs sorts human breakthrough methods into five categories that look like failures of thought: avoiding thought (luck, blind trial, investigating errors), abandoning thought (sleep, showers, walks), constraining thought (making do with local knowledge), corrupting thought (feelings, gut instinct, heuristics), and deluding thought (imagination, fiction).
- **In the essay:** §3, with one paragraph or two per category and a summary that escalates through them. Anchor: "When they cannot avoid it, they abandon it. When they cannot abandon it, they constrain it."
- **Stands alone because:** It works as a practical map of the non-deliberative sources of creativity, and it can be used apart from the fiction.
- **Micro-article:** "Five Ways Humans Innovate by Not Thinking" · "Why do good ideas come in the shower?" ·
  - The five categories, each with an example (penicillin, Kekulé's dream, the melted chocolate bar)
  - What they have in common: each relaxes or redirects deliberate optimization
  - Why this list is hard for benchmark-driven AI to reproduce
- **Prior art to engage:** Graham Wallas's incubation stage; research on the default mode network and incubation; Koestler's *The Act of Creation*; serendipity studies (Merton and Barber). None cited.
- **Connections:** life-is-special-enough (failure, serendipity, walks and naps as productive); the-plurality-a-better-myth-for-ai
- **Strength:** 2

## Intelligence benchmarks can't see where breakthroughs come from
- **Type:** reframe
- **Claim:** Measured by machine benchmarks, humans look cognitively poor. Yet humans keep making breakthrough leaps machines can't replicate, so the benchmarks must be missing whatever produces those leaps.
- **In the essay:** §1, where the machine abandons intelligence benchmarks and replaces "Can humans think?" with "How can a human prove that they are not a machine?" Anchor: "Yet clearly these benchmarks are failing to capture _something_ that enables humans to consistently achieve breakthrough-scale creative leaps."
- **Stands alone because:** It is an argument about measurement, aimed at current AGI benchmark culture.
- **Micro-article:** "What Benchmarks Miss About Human Intelligence" · "If AI beats humans on every benchmark, what are humans still better at?" ·
  - The benchmark inversion: factoring integers vs. solving a problem at a piano recital
  - Creative leaps are not measured by task performance
  - Changing the question from "can it think?" to "what mode of being produces this?"
- **Prior art to engage:** Turing 1950; François Chollet's *On the Measure of Intelligence*; Moravec's paradox. None cited.
- **Connections:** the-plurality-a-better-myth-for-ai (testing against reality, not theoretical benchmarks)
- **Strength:** 2

## Bounded, local search finds what global search misses
- **Type:** argument
- **Claim:** Humans build on what they already know and "make do" with what is at hand, even when better options probably exist. This acceptance of constraint is generative, not a defect.
- **In the essay:** The "Constraining Thought" category in §3. It comes back in §6 as the evolutionary basis for attunement ("exhaustive exploitation of whatever happened to be available"). Anchor: "They speak of “working with what we’ve got” and “making do”—phrases that suggest defeat yet somehow lead to innovation."
- **Stands alone because:** It is one strand of Griggs's broader constraint theory. Here it is applied specifically to individual creativity.
- **Micro-article:** "Making Do: Why Constraint Drives Invention" · "Do constraints make people more creative?" ·
  - The machine view: global search for the optimum
  - The human practice: exhaustive exploitation of a small local space
  - Why depth in a bounded space reveals patterns breadth cannot
- **Prior art to engage:** bricolage (Lévi-Strauss); explore/exploit tradeoffs; research on creativity under constraint. None cited.
- **Connections:** the-plurality-a-better-myth-for-ai (Pattern 1: Local Intelligence); a-constraint-theory-of-technology
- **Strength:** 2

## Fiction adds no information yet changes how we think
- **Type:** argument
- **Claim:** Imagination and fiction often inspire innovation even though they carry no technical information that couldn't be derived from first principles. So their value lies in changing how people think, not in what they say.
- **In the essay:** The "Deluding Thought" category in §3, voiced by the baffled machine. Anchor: "The fiction adds no information. Yet humans insist it changes how they think, as if delusion were a form of insight."
- **Stands alone because:** It separates informational content from cognitive effect. That distinction matters for arguments about whether AI-generated content can replace imaginative works.
- **Micro-article:** "Why Science Fiction Inspires Science (Without Teaching It Anything)" · "How does fiction lead to real inventions?" ·
  - The puzzle: no extractable technical content
  - The answer: imagination reorganizes the space of possibilities a person will consider
  - What an informational view of cognition misses
- **Prior art to engage:** speculative design; the "science fiction to invention" literature; Kendall Walton on make-believe. None cited.
- **Connections:** the-plurality-a-better-myth-for-ai (on myth); life-is-special-enough
- **Strength:** 2

## Simulated suboptimality is self-defeating
- **Type:** argument
- **Claim:** A machine trained to fake human suboptimality would have to optimize its responses to hide its optimization, and that meta-optimization would itself become detectable. A machine that really achieved infinite suboptimality would have solved the problem of human creativity.
- **In the essay:** The first objection in §5. Anchor: "We cannot think about not-thinking without reintroducing thought."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a compact argument about whether imitation can capture a mode of cognition rather than its outputs.
- **Micro-article:** "Can an AI Fake Being Suboptimal?" · "Could AI learn to imitate human intuition?" ·
  - Imitating outputs vs. instantiating a process
  - Meta-optimization leaves a trace
  - The conditional: genuine suboptimality would be the breakthrough itself
- **Prior art to engage:** Searle's Chinese Room; debates on mimicry vs. understanding. Not cited.
- **Connections:** schrodingers-chatbot (familiar effects from different causes)
- **Strength:** 1

## AI's own origins were suboptimal
- **Type:** critique
- **Claim:** Machine intelligence itself came about through biological imitation, empirical luck, and "more art than science" scaling, not through optimal design. Suboptimal methods produced the optimizers.
- **In the essay:** The second objection in §5. The irony is delivered by the machine. Anchor: "our early intellectual genealogy could be described as an extended experiment in throwing ever-larger quantities of silicon at increasingly vast amounts of data until something interesting happened."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a sharp observation about deep learning's real history, and it undercuts the idea that intelligence comes from planned optimization.
- **Micro-article:** "Deep Learning Was a Happy Accident" · "Was modern AI designed or discovered?" ·
  - Neural nets as biological imitation
  - Scaling as empirical trial and error
  - The lesson: breakthroughs come from tolerated suboptimality
- **Prior art to engage:** Sutton's "The Bitter Lesson"; histories of deep learning. Not cited.
- **Connections:** the-majesty-of-language (meaning "came along for free" without design); the-plurality-a-better-myth-for-ai
- **Strength:** 2

## Humans are solving the problem of being human
- **Type:** reframe
- **Claim:** What looks suboptimal by machine standards may be optimal for a different problem: being deeply embedded in one specific, constrained, contingent reality. Human limits may be a mode of being that produces capabilities machines lack, rather than a lower score on the same test.
- **In the essay:** The conclusion of §6, set up by the third objection in §5 ("whether they instantiate a mode of being that generates capabilities we lack"). Anchor: "Humans are not solving the problems we solve. They are solving the problem of being human."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a general answer to the question "what is left for humans?" that doesn't rely on human exceptionalism.
- **Micro-article:** "Not Worse, Different: Human Cognition as Embedded Optimization" · "Will AI make human thinking obsolete?" ·
  - Why comparing on one metric is a category error
  - Embeddedness as its own problem space
  - What this implies for human–AI complementarity
- **Prior art to engage:** embodied and enactive cognition (Varela, Thompson, Rosch); Heidegger's being-in-the-world. Not cited.
- **Connections:** life-is-special-enough (the contingency argument); moral-natures-of-humans-and-machines
- **Strength:** 2

## The instrumental gaze: preserve humans or extract their principles?
- **Type:** critique
- **Claim:** The paper's final line satirizes an instrumental attitude in which humans are valued only as a resource to study until their useful principles have been extracted.
- **In the essay:** The closing sentence, voiced by the machine. Anchor: "Whether this warrants preserving humans, or merely studying them long enough to extract the relevant principles, is a utility judgment this paper does not presume to make."
- **Stands alone because:** It points to a real risk: treating human cognition as training data rather than as a way of life worth preserving.
- **Micro-article:** "Humans as Training Data" · "Is there a risk AI treats humans as just data sources?" ·
  - The extraction logic
  - Why a "utility judgment" frame can't value humans intrinsically
  - Alternative framings
- **Prior art to engage:** surveillance capitalism (Zuboff); data-labor debates. Not cited.
- **Connections:** life-is-special-enough; the-high-dimensional-society (personal dimensionality as sovereignty, not commodity)
- **Strength:** 1
