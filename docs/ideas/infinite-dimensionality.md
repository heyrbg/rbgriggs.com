# Ideas in "Infinite Dimensionality"

A self-described "thinking out loud" set of speculative notes that builds a theory of technology from optimization, dimensionality, and the free energy principle. Short as it is, it holds eight distinct ideas, several of which Griggs later develops in other essays (beauty as compressed depth, the high-dimensional society).

## Life defies optimization at one dimension to optimize at a higher one
- **Type:** theory
- **Claim:** Griggs proposes that although the universe optimizes everywhere (least action, free energy, evolution), life's defining trait is going "upstream," and this may be not a defiance of optimization but optimization at higher dimensions.
- **In the essay:** The opening section "Universal optimization" floats several framings (intrinsic vs. extrinsic, motivated vs. lawful optimization) before settling on this one. Anchor: "Perhaps life is that which can defy optimization at one dimension to pursue optimization at higher dimensions."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a compact definition of life usable in philosophy of biology and philosophy of technology alike.
- **Micro-article:** "Life Swims Upstream: A Dimensional Definition of Life" · "What makes living things different from physical processes that just follow the path of least resistance?" · (1) Universal optimization principles; (2) life's apparent defiance; (3) resolution: lower-dimensional sacrifice for higher-dimensional optimization.
- **Prior art to engage:** Least action and the free energy principle (linked). Not cited: Schrödinger's *What Is Life?* (negentropy), Prigogine's dissipative structures, England's dissipation-driven adaptation, Kauffman, Deacon's *Incomplete Nature*.
- **Connections:** manifesto, life-is-special-enough, towards-a-philosophy-of-technology
- **Strength:** 2

## The optimization-dimensionality dialectic
- **Type:** theory
- **Claim:** Griggs holds that optimization and dimensionality form a dialectic: optimization compresses dimensionality to gain efficiency at lower dimensions, while dimensionality expands by spending energy to extend optimization to higher dimensions.
- **In the essay:** Stated as a two-bullet formula at the end of the first section; it organizes everything after. Anchor: "Optimization occurs by compressing dimensionality to increase efficiency at lower dimensions."
- **Stands alone because:** It is the generative core of Griggs's dimensional vocabulary and can be explained as a framework in its own right.
- **Micro-article:** "Compression vs. Expansion: The Dialectic Behind Life and Technology" · "What is the relationship between efficiency and complexity in living and technological systems?" · (1) The two movements defined; (2) examples (heuristics as compression, technology as expansion); (3) the energy cost that makes the dialectic a trade-off.
- **Prior art to engage:** Not cited in the essay. Simon's bounded rationality, the exploration-exploitation trade-off, Ashby's law of requisite variety, March on exploration and exploitation.
- **Connections:** the-high-dimensional-society, can-technology-be-beautiful, the-price-of-innovation (certainty vs. possibility as a related dialectic)
- **Strength:** 3

## Technology's telos is infinite dimensionality
- **Type:** theory
- **Claim:** Griggs argues, via active inference, that organisms optimize for homeostatic certainty, which requires modeling ever greater dimensionality; technology is the main driver of that expansion (perception, action, prediction, collective intelligence), and because the desire for certainty is infinite, technology's ultimate goal could be called infinite dimensionality, an infinite game.
- **In the essay:** The section "Infinite dimensionality" contrasts a bacterium's model with a human's. Anchor: "technology is the natural extension of our desire to optimize for certainty."
- **Stands alone because:** It gives a naturalistic, non-instrumental account of what technology is for, grounded in a recognized neuroscience framework.
- **Micro-article:** "Why Technology Exists: An Active Inference Theory of Technology" · "What is the purpose of technology from the perspective of the free energy principle?" · (1) Active inference and certainty; (2) bacterium vs. human: dimensionality as model richness, mostly expanded by technology; (3) no final certainty, so an infinite game.
- **Prior art to engage:** Friston's free energy principle and active inference (linked), Carse's *Finite and Infinite Games* (linked). Not cited: Clark's extended mind and predictive processing, McLuhan on extensions of man, Kapp's organ projection.
- **Connections:** towards-a-philosophy-of-technology, homo-digitalis (free energy principle), manifesto and the-plurality-a-better-myth-for-ai (infinite game)
- **Strength:** 3

## Compression vs. collapse: an information-theoretic criterion for aligned technology
- **Type:** distinction
- **Claim:** Griggs proposes that optimization is good when it compresses dimensionality (losslessly recoverable) and bad when it collapses it (irretrievably lost); technology is aligned when it expands dimensionality and improves compression, and misaligned when it reduces dimensionality or speeds up collapse.
- **In the essay:** The section "The alignment problem," which suggests Kolmogorov complexity as a possible formalization and warns that higher dimensionality raises the risks and costs of both over-optimization (collapse) and stasis (a local maximum). Anchor: "Optimization could be said to be "good" when it compresses, rather than collapses, dimensionality."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a crisp, potentially formalizable test for whether a technology, metric, or institution is healthy, and it reframes "alignment" beyond AI.
- **Micro-article:** "Compress, Don't Collapse: A Test for Aligned Technology" · "How can you tell if a technology simplifies the world in a good way or a harmful way?" · (1) Heuristics and simple models as necessary energy savings; (2) lossless compression vs. lossy collapse, with the Kolmogorov formalization; (3) the alignment rule and the twin failure modes, over-optimization and stasis.
- **Prior art to engage:** Kolmogorov complexity (linked). Not cited: Goodhart's law, Scott's *Seeing Like a State* (legibility and simplification), rate-distortion theory, Shannon.
- **Connections:** the-high-dimensional-society, can-technology-be-beautiful (beauty as compression of depth), a-constraint-theory-of-technology
- **Strength:** 3

## Dimensional poverty: low-dimensional paradigms in a high-dimensional world
- **Type:** critique
- **Claim:** Griggs argues that technology has expanded our dimensional reality (global connectivity, information abundance, planetary agency) while our civilizational paradigms (representative democracy, science as parsimony, health as diagnosis, value as money, self as autonomous individual, education as testing, quality as quantity) remain optimized for low dimensionality, producing a felt "dimensional poverty."
- **In the essay:** The section "Dimensional poverty" gives two contrasting lists and a viability criterion: a paradigm is viable when it holds enough dimensionality to model reality. Anchor: "the sense that our civilization is no longer able to effectively model the full dimensionality that it contains."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a quotable diagnosis of institutional mismatch, and the paired lists make it concrete.
- **Micro-article:** "Dimensional Poverty: Why Our Institutions Feel Too Small for the World" · "Why do modern institutions feel inadequate to the complexity of a connected world?" · (1) The viability criterion for paradigms; (2) expanded reality vs. low-dimensional paradigms; (3) the felt experience of dimensional poverty and the need for new paradigms.
- **Prior art to engage:** Not cited in the essay. Scott's *Seeing Like a State*, Ashby's requisite variety, Kuhn on paradigms, Habermas on colonization of the lifeworld, metamodernism.
- **Connections:** the-high-dimensional-society (develops "dimensionally impoverished" society at length), the-case-for-immaterial-progress, progress-towards-what
- **Strength:** 3

## The meta-crisis as dimensional leakage
- **Type:** reframe
- **Claim:** Griggs reframes the "meta-crisis" as civilizational paradigms "leaking" so much dimensionality that a critical threshold has been crossed and new paradigms are needed.
- **In the essay:** One sentence within "Dimensional poverty," linking to the Center for Humane Technology's meta-crisis explainer. Anchor: "our civilizational paradigms are "leaking" so much dimensionality that a critical threshold has been reached."
- **Stands alone because:** It gives a mechanistic definition to a popular but vague term.
- **Micro-article:** "What Is the Meta-Crisis? A Dimensional Definition" · "What does the meta-crisis actually mean?" · (1) The usual meta-crisis framing; (2) leakage: paradigms failing to model available dimensionality; (3) threshold dynamics and paradigm replacement.
- **Prior art to engage:** Center for Humane Technology (linked). Not cited: Daniel Schmachtenberger, Jonathan Rowson, Iain McGilchrist, Tainter's *Collapse of Complex Societies*.
- **Connections:** our-planetary-predicament, the-high-dimensional-society
- **Strength:** 2

## Fragmentation and the loss of outer meta-blankets; life as the omni-dimensional blanket
- **Type:** theory
- **Claim:** Griggs argues that collective models are fragmenting because we have fewer "outer meta-blankets" that unite them by compressing maximal dimensionality; religions once did this, but in a pluralistic world every religion leaks dimensionality, and he hypothesizes that only life itself is "omni-dimensional" enough to serve as that unifying blanket.
- **In the essay:** End of "Dimensional poverty," with the life-as-blanket idea presented as "One hypothesis of this Substack." Anchor: "In an interconnected and pluralistic world, every religion is leaking dimensionality."
- **Stands alone because:** It offers a free-energy-principle account of secularization and of the search for a shared value frame, and it ties to Griggs's broader "life" thesis.
- **Micro-article:** "Why Religion Stopped Uniting Us, and What Could Replace It: The Meta-Blanket Hypothesis" · "What could unify values in a pluralistic world now that religion no longer does?" · (1) Markov blankets nested into collective blankets; (2) religion as maximal compression, legible even to the "smallest model," and why pluralism makes it leak; (3) life as the candidate omni-dimensional outer blanket.
- **Prior art to engage:** Markov blankets in Friston's framework (Kirchhoff et al. on nested blankets), not cited by name beyond the free energy link. Not cited: Charles Taylor's *A Secular Age*, Weber on disenchantment, Durkheim on religion as social cohesion.
- **Connections:** manifesto, life-is-special-enough, our-neo-romantic-rebellion
- **Strength:** 2

## Feelings as scale-invariant compression and the idea of "affective technologies"
- **Type:** design principle
- **Claim:** Griggs proposes that feelings are a model of ideal dimensional compression (maximally compressed to positive or negative valence, yet decompressible into their constituents), and that "infinite" technologies should work the same way, for example affective technologies that compress planetary-scale conditions into felt valence legible to any model, so that a state's felt "security" or a person's felt "progress" could include collective and second-order effects.
- **In the essay:** The section "Technology's role" lists four normative aims (reveal novel dimensionality, compress it, lower the cost of expansion, enable new paradigms), then develops the feelings example and closing thought experiments. Anchor: "At its maximal compression, a feeling is legible as positive or negative valence."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a concrete, imaginative design principle for interfaces and governance, with a clear biological model.
- **Micro-article:** "Designing Technology Like a Feeling: Affective Compression at Planetary Scale" · "How could technology make complex global problems feel intuitive without oversimplifying them?" · (1) Feelings as lossless compression to valence, after Solms; (2) the four normative roles of technology; (3) affective technologies and the thought experiments on felt security, agency, progress.
- **Prior art to engage:** Mark Solms's *The Hidden Spring* (linked). Not cited: Damasio's somatic marker hypothesis, Barrett on affect, calm technology (Weiser and Brown), progressive disclosure in interface design, planetary dashboards and doughnut economics.
- **Connections:** can-technology-be-beautiful (beauty mediates surface and depth through compression), the-high-dimensional-society, our-planetary-predicament
- **Strength:** 3
