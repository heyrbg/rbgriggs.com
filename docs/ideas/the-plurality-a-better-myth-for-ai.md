# Ideas in "The Plurality: a Better Myth for AI"

A critique of the Singularity as AI's founding myth, followed by an alternative myth, "the Plurality", grounded in how intelligence actually produces novelty at scale. It holds about eleven distinct ideas: a theory of constraints, a four-stage lifecycle of intelligence, several critiques, and design principles.

## Myths are judged by what they make possible
- **Type:** reframe
- **Claim:** Griggs argues that the Singularity should be assessed as a founding myth, not as a forecast. Judged that way it succeeded, because it catalyzed the AI movement. Its success is also why it now needs to be replaced.
- **In the essay:** "The myth of the Singularity" section, which lists the myth's elements (Promethean transcendence, Faustian bargains, "the myth that ends all myths"). Anchor: "Myths aren't judged by factual accuracy—they're judged by **what they make possible**."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a meta-argument about how to criticize guiding narratives in technology without dismissing them.
- **Micro-article:** "The Singularity Is a Myth, and That's Not an Insult" · "Why does the AI industry believe in the Singularity?" ·
  - The Singularity's mythic structure
  - The success criterion: it catalyzed a movement
  - Why a successful myth can become an obstacle
- **Prior art to engage:** Joseph Campbell; Mircea Eliade; critiques of the Singularity as secular eschatology (e.g., the TESCREAL debate); Vinge and Kurzweil. Not cited; the essay links LessWrong and Sam Altman's "The Merge".
- **Connections:** our-neo-romantic-rebellion; progress-towards-what
- **Strength:** 2

## Infinite intelligence works only in closed, verifiable systems
- **Type:** distinction
- **Claim:** The Singularity treats intelligence as heading toward omniscience (the map becomes the territory) and omnipotence (intelligence becomes power). That picture holds for closed systems with verifiable solutions and breaks down in open-ended reality, where solutions can only be verified by trying them.
- **In the essay:** "Infinity and its limits", with AlphaGo and medical insights given as the cases where it holds. Anchor: "If you're navigating a _closed_ system with _verifiable_ solutions, then it's possible to **scale** your way to dramatic insights"
- **Stands alone because:** The closed/open distinction is a sharp, reusable test for when scaling arguments apply.
- **Micro-article:** "Where Scaling Works and Where It Doesn't" · "Can AI scale its way to superintelligence?" ·
  - The divine attributes of infinite intelligence
  - Closed, verifiable domains (games, code) vs. open reality
  - Scale in open systems: fragility, stasis, homogenization
- **Prior art to engage:** Hayek on the knowledge problem; Popper on the unpredictability of knowledge growth; debates about verifiable-reward RL. Not cited.
- **Connections:** a-constraint-theory-of-technology; the-reverse-turing-test
- **Strength:** 3

## Intelligence can't be both infinitely generative and infinitely powerful
- **Type:** argument
- **Claim:** In complex open systems, any truly new thing can exceed and change the conditions that produced it. So an intelligence that is really generative can't also predict and control everything. The Singularity's two promises contradict each other.
- **In the essay:** The closing of "Infinity and its limits". Anchor: "intelligence cannot be both infinitely generative _and_ infinitely powerful."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a compact impossibility argument against omnipotent superintelligence.
- **Micro-article:** "The Singularity's Contradiction" · "Is superintelligence even logically possible?" ·
  - Generativity means producing what exceeds its origin
  - Power means prediction and control
  - Why open systems force a choice between them
- **Prior art to engage:** Popper's argument that future knowledge can't be predicted; Stuart Kauffman's "adjacent possible"; complexity theory. Not cited.
- **Connections:** infinite-dimensionality; manifesto
- **Strength:** 3

## The only existence proofs of novelty at scale look nothing like the Singularity
- **Type:** argument
- **Claim:** Evolution, science, and culture are the only known systems that produce real novelty at scale. None of them predicts, scales, or optimizes. Each works through variation, communities criticizing each other, and local constraint.
- **In the essay:** A three-part list in "Infinity and its limits", followed by three lessons (continuous iteration, networks, transformed constraints). Anchor: "every existence proof we have of intelligence generating true novelty at scale—evolution, scientific progress, human culture—**looks** _**nothing**_ **like infinite intelligence**"
- **Stands alone because:** It is an empirical argument about where generative intelligence actually comes from.
- **Micro-article:** "What Evolution, Science, and Culture Teach Us About Intelligence" · "Where does real novelty come from?" ·
  - The three existence proofs
  - What they have in common: iteration, networks, constraint
  - Implications for AI architecture
- **Prior art to engage:** universal Darwinism; Popper and Kuhn on science; Joseph Henrich's *The Secret of Our Success*. Not cited.
- **Connections:** life-is-special-enough; the-case-for-immaterial-progress
- **Strength:** 2

## Two foundational constraints: inescapable contingency and irreducible difference
- **Type:** theory
- **Claim:** Every adaptive intelligence is shaped by a context it can't fully control (contingency). That makes every perspective partial and impossible to fully generalize (difference). Together these constraints make intelligence possible rather than limiting it.
- **In the essay:** "A new myth: The Plurality". The two constraints are defined and called "operating conditions". Anchor: "They don't limit intelligence—they **enable** it."
- **Stands alone because:** It is the core theoretical claim of the Plurality and can be stated and defended on its own.
- **Micro-article:** "Why Constraints Make Intelligence Possible" · "Are limits good for intelligence?" ·
  - Contingency: intelligence is always situated
  - Difference: partiality creates dependence on others
  - Constraints as engines rather than limits
- **Prior art to engage:** situated cognition (Lucy Suchman); standpoint epistemology; Isaiah Berlin's value pluralism; Hannah Arendt on plurality. Not cited.
- **Connections:** a-constraint-theory-of-technology; life-is-special-enough (contingency argument); our-neo-romantic-rebellion (plurality)
- **Strength:** 3

## The four-pattern lifecycle of adaptive intelligence
- **Type:** theory
- **Claim:** Adaptive intelligence moves through a cycle with four stages, each creating the conditions for the next. Local intelligence discovers, collective intelligence generates, proven intelligence validates, and evolving intelligence perpetuates. The cycle spirals outward instead of converging.
- **In the essay:** "The patterns of plurality" (one subsection per pattern) and "A new cosmology of intelligence" (the cycle). Anchor: "The cycle never converges but forever **spirals** outward, each revolution opening domains that couldn't be imagined at previous levels."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a full model with a clear structure.
- **Micro-article:** "The Four Stages of Adaptive Intelligence" · "How does intelligence actually grow over time?" ·
  - Local → collective → proven → evolving
  - How each stage feeds the next
  - Event (the Singularity) vs. process (the Plurality)
- **Prior art to engage:** Donald Campbell's blind variation and selective retention; Popper's conjecture and refutation; collective intelligence research (Thomas Malone); ecology. Not cited.
- **Connections:** progress-towards-what; infinite-dimensionality
- **Strength:** 3

## Constraint builds taste; slop is the entropy tax of scale without judgment
- **Type:** concept
- **Claim:** Testing ideas against the same limits over and over develops taste and contextual judgment, the heuristics that keep good ideas and drop bad ones however they arose. AI that has scale but no taste produces "slop".
- **In the essay:** Pattern 1 (Local Intelligence: the master craftsperson, the scientist who "senses" promising directions) and "The future of intelligence is plural". Anchor (link markup removed): "Slop is the **entropy tax** of scale without judgement—infinite ideas decaying toward meaninglessness."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a crisp, quotable diagnosis of AI slop that also says how judgment forms.
- **Micro-article:** "Why AI Has Ideas but No Taste" · "Why does AI generate so much mediocre content?" ·
  - Taste as a product of repeated engagement with constraint
  - Generation is cheap; recognition is scarce
  - Slop as entropy
- **Prior art to engage:** Polanyi's tacit knowledge; Dreyfus's model of skill acquisition; Richard Sennett's *The Craftsman*. Not cited.
- **Connections:** the-reverse-turing-test (heuristics, gut feeling, attunement); can-technology-be-beautiful
- **Strength:** 3

## The best optimization strategy keeps the suboptimal
- **Type:** argument
- **Claim:** Intelligence that keeps evolving preserves what looks wasteful (competitive tension, variations, redundancy), because those tensions create anti-fragility that pure optimization would remove.
- **In the essay:** Pattern 4 (Evolving Intelligence), with a set of paradoxes. Anchor: "**Paradoxical tensions create anti-fragility that optimization will erase.**"
- **Stands alone because:** It is a general principle for robust systems, with uses well beyond AI.
- **Micro-article:** "Why Evolution Keeps the Waste" · "Is optimization bad for long-term resilience?" ·
  - Redundancy and variation as insurance against what wasn't anticipated
  - Adaptation instead of planning
  - "Staying perpetually capable of surprise"
- **Prior art to engage:** Nassim Taleb's antifragility (the term is used, Taleb isn't cited); exploration/exploitation; ecological resilience (C.S. Holling).
- **Connections:** the-reverse-turing-test (infinite suboptimality); the-high-dimensional-society (ADI "Structurally Sub-Optimal"); life-is-special-enough
- **Strength:** 2

## General intelligence becomes a commodity; embedded agents get the edge
- **Type:** prediction
- **Claim:** As returns from pure scale shrink, general intelligence will become a commodity. The value will go to agents that are deeply embedded in a problem, have the taste to recognize good solutions, and can work with other minds.
- **In the essay:** "The future of intelligence is plural". It cites diminishing returns from frontier training runs and the lack of AI novelty beyond the training set. Anchor: '"General intelligence" is looking more like a **commodity**, available everywhere.'
- **Stands alone because:** It is a concrete, checkable prediction about AI economics.
- **Micro-article:** "When General Intelligence Is a Commodity" · "Where will value come from in AI if models become commodities?" ·
  - Diminishing returns from scale
  - Embeddedness, taste, and social capability as what sets agents apart
  - What this means for builders
- **Prior art to engage:** model commoditization debates; Hayek on local knowledge. Not cited.
- **Connections:** a-constraint-theory-of-technology
- **Strength:** 2

## Social intelligence is the new superintelligence; safety is negotiation, not alignment
- **Type:** design principle
- **Claim:** Griggs proposes four design shifts: local expertise over universal knowledge, social coordination over isolated scaling, testing against reality over benchmarks, and dynamic negotiation over static alignment. He argues the only safe AI is one that knows its perspective is partial and negotiates differences with other minds.
- **In the essay:** "Designing a future worth building", framed by the coming flood of "trillions of agents" with no conception of social intelligence. Anchor: "The only safe AI is one that recognizes its partial perspective and seeks to negotiate differences amongst other minds."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a pointed alternative to value-alignment approaches to AI safety.
- **Micro-article:** "Beyond Alignment: Safety as Negotiation" · "Is aligning AI to human values the right approach to AI safety?" ·
  - Why a static alignment target fails in a plural world
  - Agents that treat other minds as partners rather than obstacles
  - Four design shifts
- **Prior art to engage:** cooperative AI (Dafoe et al.); multi-agent systems; social choice approaches to alignment; deliberative democracy. Not cited.
- **Connections:** the-high-dimensional-society (ADI translation and coordination); our-planetary-predicament (coordination)
- **Strength:** 3

## The Plurality's own infinity: an infinite game rather than an event
- **Type:** reframe
- **Claim:** The Singularity imagines one event of takeoff toward certainty. The Plurality is a continuous process that achieves its own kind of infinity by endlessly regenerating possibility within constraints.
- **In the essay:** "A new cosmology of intelligence" and the conclusion. Anchor: "the most _infinite_ form of intelligence is the one that keeps discovering new ways to play an infinite game."
- **Stands alone because:** It takes the language of infinity back from Singularity discourse.
- **Micro-article:** "Two Infinities: Singularity vs. Plurality" · "Can intelligence be infinite without a Singularity?" ·
  - Event vs. process
  - Infinity by conquest vs. infinity by regeneration
  - Four billion years of evidence
- **Prior art to engage:** James Carse, *Finite and Infinite Games* (phrase used, not cited)
- **Connections:** infinite-dimensionality; manifesto (infinite game)
- **Strength:** 2
