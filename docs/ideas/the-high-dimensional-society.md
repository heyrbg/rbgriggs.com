# Ideas in "The High-Dimensional Society"

A four-part argument: modern society's malaise comes from coordinating at scale through low-dimensional proxies, and AI that can navigate meaning could support a "high-dimensional society" if it is built under strict structural constraints. It is one of the densest essays in the corpus, with about twelve distinct ideas.

## Blaming technology is a category error
- **Type:** reframe
- **Claim:** Griggs argues that modern society is "a miracle and a mess", and that blaming technology for the mess confuses symptom and cause. Technology runs on the same broken operating system of large-scale coordination as everything else, so the fix is better technology that changes that operating system.
- **In the essay:** The opening of Part 1. Anchor: "The problem with technology is that it runs on the same broken operating system as everything else."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a clear counter-position to techlash and "log off" arguments.
- **Micro-article:** "Don't Blame the Tools: Society's Broken Operating System" · "Is technology why modern life feels empty?" ·
  - Miracle and mess
  - Why "log off" mistakes symptom for cause
  - Targeting the coordination layer
- **Prior art to engage:** Jonathan Haidt; Neil Postman; Jacques Ellul; techlash literature. Not cited.
- **Connections:** the-question-concerning-digital-technology; our-planetary-predicament; what-does-a-good-digital-life-look
- **Strength:** 2

## Abstraction: the stupid, brilliant trick that lets society scale
- **Type:** theory
- **Claim:** Strangers coordinate by recognizing the same simple proxies (price, vote, credential, click), not by understanding each other. Proxies don't just compress reality, they transform it, and every society makes the wager that individual complexity is worth sacrificing for collective capacity.
- **In the essay:** "The Stupid, Brilliant Trick". Anchor: "This is the wager every society makes: individual complexity sacrificed for collective capacity."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a compact theory of how large-scale coordination works and what it costs.
- **Micro-article:** "The Stupid, Brilliant Trick: How Proxies Make Society Possible" · "How do millions of strangers cooperate?" ·
  - Recognition instead of understanding
  - Proxies transform reality (supply curves, price discovery)
  - The wager
- **Prior art to engage:** James C. Scott, *Seeing Like a State* (echoed in "color-blind bureaucrats"); Hayek on the price system; Weber on bureaucracy; Luhmann's media of communication. Not cited.
- **Connections:** our-planetary-predicament (coordination); a-constraint-theory-of-technology
- **Strength:** 3

## From proxy to prison: how optimization overfits society
- **Type:** theory
- **Claim:** Once you have a proxy you have a score, then a game, then optimization. Dimensions the proxy can't see must survive against the gradient, sustained by outside sources like religion and tradition. When that energy runs out, the system overfits and those dimensions become invisible, then inconvenient, then extinct.
- **In the essay:** "From Proxy to Prison". Anchor: "Once you have a proxy, you have a score. Once you have a score, you have a game. And once you have a game, people start playing to win."
- **Stands alone because:** It gives Goodhart's law a mechanism and a time course, using the vocabulary of machine learning.
- **Micro-article:** "How Society Overfits" · "Why do metrics end up ruining what they measure?" ·
  - Proxy → score → game → optimization
  - Fighting the gradient, and the energy it takes
  - Invisible, inconvenient, extinct
- **Prior art to engage:** Goodhart's law; Campbell's law; Jerry Muller, *The Tyranny of Metrics*; C. Thi Nguyen on value capture. Not cited.
- **Connections:** the-plurality-a-better-myth-for-ai (scale leading to homogenization); infinite-dimensionality (over-optimization as collapse)
- **Strength:** 3

## Dimensional poverty
- **Type:** concept
- **Claim:** Dimensional poverty is the felt sense that one's potential far exceeds what society can actualize. It is the exhaustion of forcing a high-dimensional self into a low-dimensional world. Modern technology deepens it by showing more ways of being than society can sustain.
- **In the essay:** "Dimensional Poverty". Anchor: "Dimensional poverty is the felt sense that the potential you hold contains so much more than what society could ever hope to actualize."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a named, defined concept for a widely felt malaise, and good material for AI search.
- **Micro-article:** "What Is Dimensional Poverty?" · "Why do I feel reduced to metrics even when I'm succeeding?" ·
  - The definition
  - Self-compression and the ache of foreclosure
  - Why more options make it worse
- **Prior art to engage:** Marcuse's *One-Dimensional Man*; Weber's iron cage; Hartmut Rosa on resonance and alienation. Not cited.
- **Connections:** infinite-dimensionality (where the term also appears)
- **Strength:** 3

## Label proxies vs. interface proxies (thickening the proxy)
- **Type:** distinction
- **Claim:** Current proxies compress, sorting complexity into labels or ranking it into scalars. A proxy could instead be an interface that maps where you are in a space of possibilities and what you are near. With thicker proxies, price, careers, and education could hold many dimensions at once.
- **In the essay:** "A Different Kind of Proxy", with worked examples (multi-dimensional price, a coffee shop discount to preserve the lunch vibe, opportunity that finds you, learning made legible without credentials). Anchor: "A label asks: _which box do you fit in?_ An interface asks: _where are you in the space of possibilities, and what are you near?_" (Original formatting splits this across a line break.)
- **Stands alone because:** It is a crisp design distinction for any system of measurement or coordination.
- **Micro-article:** "Labels vs. Interfaces: Thicker Proxies for a Richer Society" · "Could AI replace résumés, credentials, and simple prices?" ·
  - Labels and scalars as compression
  - Interfaces as maps
  - Worked examples: price, career, education
- **Prior art to engage:** Glen Weyl and Audrey Tang on plurality and quadratic mechanisms; recommender-systems research; Clifford Geertz on thick description. Not cited.
- **Connections:** the-majesty-of-language (embedding space); can-technology-be-beautiful (compression vs. collapse)
- **Strength:** 3

## Flood optimization, don't fight it
- **Type:** design principle
- **Claim:** The problem isn't that we optimize, it is that we optimize on too little. Saturating proxies with context changes the gradient, so what people care about becomes part of what the system optimizes for.
- **In the essay:** "Optimize All the Things". Anchor: "We don’t fight optimization. **We flood it.** We don’t destroy the old proxies. **We drown them in context.**"
- **Stands alone because:** It is a counterintuitive, quotable alternative to anti-optimization positions.
- **Micro-article:** "Don't Fight Optimization, Flood It" · "Is optimization the problem with modern society?" ·
  - Optimizing on too little
  - Expanding the model to fit reality
  - Changing the gradient
- **Prior art to engage:** multi-objective optimization; Goodhart's-law mitigation; Stuart Russell's critique of fixed objectives. Not cited.
- **Connections:** the-plurality-a-better-myth-for-ai; the-reverse-turing-test (contrast: the limits of optimization)
- **Strength:** 3

## Thicker proxies change the geometry of coordination
- **Type:** prediction
- **Claim:** When coordination can see more dimensions, structural changes follow "because the geometry changed": governance localizes (subsidiarity becomes geometric), cooperation becomes ambient, the future enters present calculations, and conflict becomes clearer as tribal bundles break into specific disagreements. Hard choices stay but can't be avoided.
- **In the essay:** Four bolded predictions in "Optimize All the Things", plus the preceding paragraph on conflict in high resolution. Anchor: "Real subsidiarity becomes not just a political ideal, but a geometric inevitability."
- **Stands alone because:** Each prediction, especially the one about breaking polarization into dimensions, could be its own short piece.
- **Micro-article:** "How Higher Dimensionality Could Defuse Polarization" · "Could AI reduce political polarization?" ·
  - Tribal bundles as low-dimensional artifacts
  - Breaking bundles apart and finding hidden consensus
  - Conflict "forced into the sunlight"
- **Prior art to engage:** subsidiarity (Catholic social teaching, EU); Coase on transaction costs; vTaiwan and Pol.is; Ostrom. Not cited.
- **Connections:** our-planetary-predicament
- **Strength:** 2

## Dimensional abundance: variance as signal, failure as information
- **Type:** concept
- **Claim:** In a high-dimensional society the work of making oneself legible would shift from the person to the system. Variance and oddity would count as signal ("the R&D department of society"), and failed ventures would keep their full value as information.
- **In the essay:** "Dimensional Abundance". Anchor: "Variance isn’t noise to filter out; it’s what distinguishes your dimensional signature from everyone else’s."
- **Stands alone because:** It is the positive counterpart to dimensional poverty and gives a concrete picture of what a better society would feel like.
- **Micro-article:** "Dimensional Abundance" · "What would a society that values individuality at scale look like?" ·
  - Relief from self-compression
  - Weirdness as information
  - Failure preserved
- **Prior art to engage:** Amartya Sen's capabilities approach; Mill on "experiments in living". Not cited.
- **Connections:** life-is-special-enough; the-case-for-immaterial-progress
- **Strength:** 2

## Proxies exist because dimensionality is expensive, and AI changes the cost
- **Type:** argument
- **Claim:** Proxies exist because holding many dimensions takes computation that grows faster than coordination can afford. Cheap computation, and machine learning that can traverse high-dimensional spaces, change that cost structure. LLMs are the existence proof that meaning can be made computationally tractable (intelligence as "dimensional translation").
- **In the essay:** The opening of Part 3, with the embedding explanation and the example of translating policy between a libertarian and a progressive. Griggs concedes that "a big gap" separates current LLMs from robust mediation. Anchor: "This is why proxies exist to begin with: to make large-scale coordination affordable."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a cost-based explanation of why social abstraction exists and why AI could change it.
- **Micro-article:** "Why Society Runs on Proxies, and Why AI Could Change That" · "How could AI change how society coordinates?" ·
  - The computational cost of dimensionality
  - Embeddings as tractable meaning
  - Dimensional translation as a new kind of intelligence
- **Prior art to engage:** Coase and transaction-cost economics; Herbert Simon; the socialist calculation debate. Not cited.
- **Connections:** the-majesty-of-language (near-identical embedding passage); infinite-dimensionality
- **Strength:** 3

## Artificial Dimensional Intelligence (ADI): perceive, compress, project, translate
- **Type:** concept
- **Claim:** ADI is intelligence as the ability to perceive and act in high-dimensional reality without compression. Its purpose is to expand the dimensionality that human judgment and coordination can reach, not to automate tasks or surpass minds. It has four mediating functions: perceive, compress holographically, project, and translate.
- **In the essay:** "Artificial Dimensional Intelligence". Anchor: "Unlike an AI built to predict or persuade, ADI is built to **reveal and relate**."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a coined term with a functional specification and a reframing of what AI is for.
- **Micro-article:** "What Is Artificial Dimensional Intelligence?" · "What if AI's purpose were coordination rather than automation?" ·
  - The definition and purpose
  - The four functions (with holographic compression: "every resolution contains the whole")
  - Making coordination smarter, not individuals
- **Prior art to engage:** Doug Engelbart's augmenting human intellect; collective intelligence; Weyl and Tang, *Plurality*; AI-mediated deliberation (e.g., DeepMind's Habermas Machine). Not cited.
- **Connections:** schrodingers-chatbot (holographic properties); the-plurality-a-better-myth-for-ai (social intelligence); whats-the-deal-with-cognitive-augmentation
- **Strength:** 3

## Three structural constraints: commons, structurally sub-optimal, transparent in function and private in substance
- **Type:** design principle
- **Claim:** A high-dimensional society can't be designed, only given conditions to emerge in. Its ADI must be an unownable, distributed commons; it must be deliberately sub-optimal, with friction and redundancy that resist monoculture; and it must be transparent in how it operates while protecting a personal "right to opacity".
- **In the essay:** Part 4, "How Do We Actually Build This Thing?" Anchor: "The interface is transparent; your life is not."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a governance framework that answers the surveillance-dystopia objection.
- **Micro-article:** "Three Rules for AI That Coordinates Society" · "How do you stop AI-coordinated society from becoming a dystopia?" ·
  - Commons, not commodity
  - Sub-optimality by design
  - Glass box for the system, right to opacity for the person
- **Prior art to engage:** Ostrom's commons governance; Édouard Glissant's "right to opacity" (phrase echoed, not cited); privacy-preserving computation; protocol-not-platform arguments (Mike Masnick). Not cited.
- **Connections:** the-plurality-a-better-myth-for-ai (preserving suboptimal tension); the-reverse-turing-test; a-constraint-theory-of-technology
- **Strength:** 3

## Preserve what cannot be optimized: mediation in order to be free of mediation
- **Type:** design principle
- **Claim:** Any powerful coordination system needs a "north star" outside itself, a firm commitment to preserve what can't be optimized. The point of mediating dimensionality is to free people to live unmediated in their closest relationships, passions, and sacred pursuits.
- **In the essay:** The closing section of Part 4. Anchor: "The entire point of mediating dimensionality is **to free us from mediation**."
- **Stands alone because:** It is a limiting principle against totalizing optimization, applicable to any technology.
- **Micro-article:** "What Technology Should Never Optimize" · "Should some parts of life be off-limits to AI?" ·
  - The danger of total systems
  - The north star outside the system
  - Mediation in service of unmediated life
- **Prior art to engage:** Borgmann's focal practices; Habermas on system vs. lifeworld; Sabbath traditions. Not cited.
- **Connections:** what-does-a-good-digital-life-look; manifesto; can-technology-be-beautiful
- **Strength:** 2
